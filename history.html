Our Story

History of Lucia Lodge

Five generations of the Harlan family have stewarded this stretch of the Big Sur coast since it was homesteaded in the 1800s. Long before Highway 1 existed, this was simply wilderness — a remote piece of the Santa Lucia Mountains, named for St. Lucy by the Spanish explorer Sebastián Vizcaíno in 1602, tumbling down to meet the Pacific.

What began as a homestead grew, over a century, into a waypoint for travelers along one of the most scenic roads in the country — and, more recently, weathered a loss that tested the family's resolve without ever closing its doors.

The Story

A Century and a Half on One Piece of Coast

1800s

The Harlan Homestead

The land is homesteaded by the pioneering Harlan family, among the first to settle this remote stretch of the Big Sur South Coast. Life here was defined by isolation — the nearest town days away by horse and trail, long before any road connected this coastline to the rest of California.

Early 1900s

Generations Take Root

The family that would come to define this coastline settled in for the long haul, raising children on the homestead through decades before Highway 1 made this stretch of coast accessible to the outside world. Self-sufficiency wasn't a choice — it was the only way to live here.

1937

The Lucia Store Opens

The Lucia store opens its doors the same year Highway 1 is completed, transforming the homestead into a waypoint for the travelers now passing through. It quickly becomes a fixture for mail, groceries, gas, and coffee — a gathering point for locals, hunters, fishermen, and road-trippers alike, on a coastline where the next stop was often an hour away.

Mid-1900s

A Lodge Grows on the Cliffs

As Highway 1 brought a steady stream of travelers south from Monterey and north from San Simeon, the lodge began to grow along the ocean-side cliffs. Cabins were added one at a time, always oriented toward the water — a design instinct that would define the property's reputation for having some of the best unobstructed views on the entire coast.

1950s–1990s

Building, Cabin by Cabin

Each generation added to what the last one built — new cabins, updated plumbing and wiring, and eventually the ten-room layout guests know today. Through it all, the rooms stayed intentionally simple: no phones, no televisions, nothing to distract from the view and the sound of the surf below.

2021

Fire, and a Family's Resolve

The original restaurant and store are lost to fire. The lodge, 100 yards north, remains open throughout — and in the same pioneering spirit as their ancestors, the Harlan family begins planning to rebuild this vital part of the Big Sur South Coast.

Today

The Fifth Generation

The fifth generation of the Harlan family welcomes guests to Lucia, a village named for the metamorphic mountains that surround it — still perched above the same surf their ancestors once looked out on, still without a phone or a television in sight.
